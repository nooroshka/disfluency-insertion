## Overview

Large language models are usually trained to produce fluent, polished text. Human speech, however, is full of natural imperfections: pauses, fillers, repetitions, and self-corrections.

This project explores a simple question:

> **Can introducing natural disfluencies make AI-generated speech sound more human?**

Inspired by the research paper *“Enhancing Naturalness in LLM-Generated Utterances through Disfluency Insertion,”* we fine-tuned language models to transform fluent text into more conversational utterances. We then synthesized speech from the generated text and evaluated how listeners perceived it.

## What We Did

1. **Disfluency insertion:** Adapted language models to introduce natural-sounding hesitations, repetitions, and self-corrections into fluent text.
2. **Speech synthesis:** Converted model-generated utterances into audio for evaluation.
3. **Human evaluation:** Conducted listening tests to examine whether participants could distinguish generated speech from human speech.
4. **Analysis:** Investigated how natural disfluencies influence perceived human-likeness.

## Key Finding

In our listening evaluation, participants **could not reliably distinguish AI-generated speech from real human speech**. This suggests that natural disfluencies may help make synthetic speech feel more human, within the conditions tested in our project.

## Research Context

**Based on:** *Enhancing Naturalness in LLM-Generated Utterances through Disfluency Insertion*

The project investigates how disfluencies—often treated as errors in speech—can instead contribute to more natural conversational AI.
